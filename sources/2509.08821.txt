                  Supplementary Note 2.    Finite-temperature properties of NCTO model under in-plane fields

   In-plane field-induced phase transitions.The magnetization process of NCTO under in-planea                             ∗ direction magnetic fields
has been a focus in recent experimental studies [27,36,40,46,55,56]. Those works have proposed several scenarios, including:
(a) a first-order transition from the (canted) zigzag-ordered AFM phase to a coexisting zigzag and spin-disordered phase at about
6 T, followed by subsequent transitions (around 8 T) to a spin-disordered phase (possibly a QSL) and finally to a spin-polarized
phase (around 10 T) [27,46]; (b) a first-order phase transition at about 6 T and then continuously to the polarized phase [81]; or
(c) a first-order transition from the triple-Q phase at about 8 T, and a quantum critical point (around 10 T) [56].
   In this work, employing the XTRG and DMRG methods with our proposed model parameters as listed in Supplementary
Table.1,  we  have  calculated  the  specific  heat  and  thermodynamic  entropy  under  in-plane  magnetic  fields  up  to  20  T.  Our
two-dimensional numerical simulations reveal only a single phase transition (no intermediate QSL phase) from the ordered
phase to a (partially) polarized phase.  Notably, the aforementioned first-order transition from “the (canted) zigzag or triple-
Q ordered phase” to a “canted AFM” or a “coexisting zigzag and spin-disordered phase” — which possibly originates from
three-dimensional material properties — remains undetectable in our two-dimensional finite-size study.
   The  landscapes  of  specific  heat,  thermal  entropy,  and  magnetizations.Supplementary  Fig.4apresents  a  landscape
of specific heatC        m.   The temperature scaleT         H  associated with the high-temperature specific heat peak remains nearly un-
