Abstract

Neural quantum states provide accurate variational representations for interacting lat-
tice fermions and spins. Turning a new ansatz into a reproducible calculation, however,
requires coordinated implementations of the Hamiltonian, sampling, optimization, ob-
servables, and accelerator parallelism.  We present LaQX (Lattice Quantum simulation
based on jaX), an open-source neural quantum state library based on JAX that organizes
these components through common functional interfaces.   Beyond ground-state vari-
ational Monte Carlo, LaQX supports excited-state calculations, Green’s Function Monte
Carlo refinement, adiabatic evolution, and real-time evolution with time-dependent vari-
ational Monte Carlo. It consolidates methods developed in recent high-accuracy neural
quantum state studies of the Hubbard model and frustrated magnets[1–3], and the pack-
age has been used to investigate the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model[4]. We
document these capabilities with reproducible calculations for Hubbard,J                    1-J 2 Heisen-
berg,  altermagnetic  Hubbard,  and  interacting  Hofstadter  models.   Function-oriented
modules separate the scientific components and limit the source context needed for ex-
tensions by human developers and large language models. Source code, documentation,
and executable examples are available athttps://github.com/bytedance/laqx.

1 Introduction

Lattice models connect microscopic Hamiltonians to collective quantum phenomena.  Their
numerical  solution  is  nevertheless  difficult  because  the  Hilbert-space  dimension  grows  ex-
ponentially with system size.  Exact diagonalization (ED) is consequently restricted to finite
clusters[5], density-matrix renormalization group[6]and matrix product state (MPS) meth-
ods[7]are most effective in one dimension and quasi-one-dimensional geometries, and higher-
dimensional tensor-network contractions require additional approximations[8,9].  Quantum
Monte Carlo[5,10–13]can reach much larger systems when a non-negative sampling weight
is available, but frustration and fermionic statistics can produce a sign problem whose generic
resolution is computationally hard[14,15]. Comparisons on paradigmatic models such as the
Hubbard model[16]therefore continue to rely on complementary numerical methods with
different controlled limits[17–19].
     Neural quantum states (NQS) add a flexible class of variational wavefunctions to this land-
scape. In variational Monte Carlo (VMC)[10,12], a neural network represents the many-body
amplitude, and its parameters are optimized using stochastic estimates over the Born distribu-
tion[20]. The field now encompasses a broad range of architectures and applications[21,22].
Early work combined restricted Boltzmann machines with conventional fermionic pair-product
states[23], while convolutional networks addressed frustrated magnets[24]and autoregres-
sive recurrent networks enabled direct sampling[25].  Subsequent developments introduced
symmetry-aware deep networks for frustrated spin systems[26], neural backflow and hidden-
fermion constructions for lattice fermions[27–29], and variational protocols for excited states
and dynamics[30,31].
     Recent studies have used hidden-fermion Pfaffian states[32], Transformer backflow[1,


                                                                 2
  SciPost Physics Codebases                                                           Submission



33], convolutional backflow[2], and symmetry-preserving pairing states[34]to investigate
the ground states of the square-latticet-t         ′ Hubbard model. Large-scale NQS calculations have
also examined topological superconductivity and quantum criticality in the triangular-lattice
Hofstadter-Hubbard model[4,35].   Combining neural networks with tensor networks pro-
vides another route to correlated variational states[3].  These studies compare energies and
order-specific correlations across variational families to distinguish competing phases.  The
range of fermionic, spin, excited-state, and dynamical applications motivates software that
can accommodate new methods without duplicating the full VMC workflow.
    Reusable software has developed alongside these methods. NetKet and jVMC provide gen-
eral NQS and VMC infrastructure[36,37], while Quantax is a JAX package for flexible lattice
many-body and NQS calculations[38]. In the complementary setting of first-quantized, real-
space electronic structure, FermiNet established the accuracy of antisymmetric neural wave-
functions[39,40],  and JaQMC provides an open JAX framework for neural-network VMC
and  diffusion  Monte  Carlo[41,42].   Together  with  recent  GPU-vectorized  tensor-network
VMC[43], these efforts show that accelerator performance depends not only on the cost of
a single wavefunction evaluation, but also on batched sampling, differentiation, and linear
algebra. They also establish the value of independent codebases with different physical scope
and extension patterns.
    Built on JAX[44], LaQX targets interacting lattice fermions and spins.  It provides neu-
ral backflow and tensor-network states, symmetry projection, Metropolis-Hastings sampling,
first-order and natural-gradient optimization, and observable evaluation. The same interfaces
support ground-state VMC with optional Green’s Function Monte Carlo (GFMC)[45,46]re-
finement, joint optimization of low-lying states with Natural Excited States (NES)[30], mea-
surements on trained states,  adiabatic evolution by variational continuation,  and real-time
evolution with time-dependent VMC (tVMC).
    LaQX consolidates the computational framework developed for neural quantum state cal-
culations of the doped Hubbard model, including Transformer-backflow states and the MARCH
optimizer[1], followed by the SCALE and ACE convolutional backflow architectures[2].  It
also incorporates architecture that combine convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with MPS,
denoted CNN-MPS, for frustrated magnets[3].  The convolutional-backflow and CNN-MPS
studies report variational energies among the best available for their respective fermionic and
spin benchmarks.  LaQX has subsequently been applied to the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-
Hubbard model, where NQS and independent projected entangled pair state (PEPS) calcula-
tions provided complementary real-space evidence of spin-charge separation[4]. The present
release brings these developments together in a documented package that supports multiple
ansatz families and physical workflows.
    The implementation is deliberately function-oriented. Wavefunctions, physical operators,
samplers, and parameter updates are connected through small interfaces, with numerical state
passed explicitly between them. This separation makes JAX transformations directly applicable
and allows many scientific extensions to remain local to one component. It also supports pro-
gramming with large language models (LLMs), including the conversational workflow known
asvibe coding[47]. Limiting cross-component dependencies reduces the source context needed
for an extension. Section3.2illustrates these extension paths.
    The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section2defines the numerical meth-
ods and notation. Section3describes the software architecture. Section4presents calculations
for Hubbard,J                    1-J  2 Heisenberg, altermagnetic, and interacting Hofstadter models.

rule.  Each component is exposed through a small functional interface, so a complete work-

Numerical state is passed explicitly between components.  This design permits compilation,
vectorization, automatic differentiation, and device parallelism to be applied to the assembled
numerical update[44].
    The state layer implements the architectures described in Sec.2.2, together with the ACE-
NES and ACE-PEFT specializations of Secs.2.4and2.7.  Independently, the operator layer
represents Hamiltonians and observables through the configurations and matrix elements con-
nected to each sampled occupation vector. Sampling and optimization then act on these com-
mon representations without depending on the internal construction of a particular ansatz or
model.
    This separation also determines the parallelization strategy. Batches of sampled configura-
tions are distributed across accelerator devices, while the variational parameters and update
state are shared. Ground-state optimization methods reuse the same outer VMC workflow, and
measurement, projection, excited-state, and dynamical calculations reuse the same state and
operator interfaces.  The architecture therefore separates physical definitions from sampling,
optimization, and distributed execution while preserving a common numerical core.

3.2Locality of changes and LLM-assisted development

Small functional interfaces also support LLM-assisted programming.  Reconstructing a large
inheritance hierarchy or tracing implicit mutable state across many files consumes additional
context and tokens, increasing the cost of a change.  In LaQX, many extensions require only
the relevant scientific component and its interface to be included in the context.
    Table1summarizes this locality at the level of scientific concepts. A new Hamiltonian de-
fines its connected configurations and matrix elements, after which the existing local-energy
and sampling machinery can be reused.  A new ansatz supplies initialization and amplitude
evaluation without changing the Hamiltonian or optimization methods.  Likewise, a new up-
date rule can operate on the common stochastic estimators without depending on the con-
struction of the state.
    The example of the altermagnetic Hubbard model in Sec.4.3provides a concrete measure
of this locality.

branch therefore pumps approximately one third of a particle per inserted flux quantum, with
a combined response of approximately one particle. This fractional response is consistent with
flux insertion connecting states within a topological ground-state manifold[59].

but it demonstrates that the Hofstadter workflows extend to interacting spinful fermions and
cross-method validation.

5 Conclusion

We have introduced LaQX, a JAX implementation of neural quantum state methods for interact-
ing lattice fermions and spins. The code combines Metropolis-Hastings sampling, several vari-
ational ansatz families, first-order and natural-gradient optimization, observables, stochastic
projection, excited states, adiabatic continuation, and real-time evolution through a common
set of functional interfaces.  The doped Hubbard and frustratedJ                     1-J  2 Heisenberg model cal-
culations connect the release to methods that have achieved high variational accuracy[1–3].
The Hofstadter-Hubbard applications encompass many-body charge pumping[59]and NQS
calculations of spin-charge separation[4].  The altermagnetic, excited-state, and dynamical
examples test the same interfaces on one-body observables, low-energy states, and real-time
evolution.
    The library separates scientific components through explicit functional interfaces.  Wave-
functions, physical operators, and update rules can therefore be extended through local source
changes.  This locality is useful for conventional development and for LLM-assisted program-
ming, where unnecessary cross-file dependencies consume a finite context window.  Source
code, documentation, and the scripts used for the calculations in this paper are available at
https://github.com/bytedance/laqx.
