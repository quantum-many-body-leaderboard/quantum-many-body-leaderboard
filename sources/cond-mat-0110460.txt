                 Using computational  techniques, it  is shown  that  pairing is  a robust  property of hole  doped anti-
                ferromagnetic  (AF)  insulators.  In  one  dimension  (1D)  and  for  two-leg  ladder  systems,  a  BCS-like
                variational  wave  function  with  long-bond  spin-singlets  and  a  Jastrow  factor  provides  an  accurate
                representation of the ground state of the t-J model, even though strong quantum ﬂuctuations destroy
                the  oﬀ-diagonal  superconducting  (SC)  long-range  order  in  this  case.   However,  in  two  dimensions
                (2D) it is argued – and numerically conﬁrmed using several techniques, especially  quantum Monte
                Carlo (QMC) – that quantum ﬂuctuations are not strong enough to suppress superconductivity.
