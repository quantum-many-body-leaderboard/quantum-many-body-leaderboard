                      Neural-network    variational    Monte    Carlo    provides    an    accurate    route    to    solving    the    electronic
                   Schrödinger   equation,   but   many   neural   wavefunctions   are   optimized   for   a   single   nuclear   geome-
                   try,  requiring  separate  calculations  to  map  out  a  potential  energy  surface.   We  introduce  NucleoNet,
                   a  nuclear-aware  determinant  ansatz  in  which  electrons  and  nuclei  form  a  unified  particle  set  propa-
                   gated  through  a  shared  electron–nucleus  backbone  that  jointly  evolves  one-particle  and  two-particle
                   representations.      Nuclei   carry   learned   representations   that   evolve   alongside   the   electronic   repre-
                   sentations   and   condition   orbital   construction,    while   antisymmetry   is   imposed   only   by   electronic
                   determinants.      On   equilibrium   small-molecule   benchmarks,    NucleoNet   achieves   accuracy   compa-
                   rable   to   established   neural-wavefunction   baselines   with   substantially   fewer   parameters.     For   each
                   molecular   system,   a   single   model   optimized   jointly   across   geometries   describes   the   N2   dissociation
                   curve,   Li   displacement   in   LiCN,   and   the   ethane   and   formamide   minimum-energy   paths   for   bond
                   dissociation.    Sparse-geometry   training   generalizes   to   unseen   geometries   for   N2   and   LiCN   without
                   further  optimization,  and  N2  ablations  show  that  nuclear  streams  substantially  improve  this  interpo-
                   lation.   These  results  show  that  a  compact  wavefunction  with  jointly  evolved  nuclear  representations
                   can  describe  the  geometries  of  a  molecular  system  with  a  single  set  of  parameters.

                                                                    D.       Determinant   Construction

   After   the   shared   electron–nucleus   backbone,   the   final   electronic   representations   are   used   to   construct   the   orbital

                                                   II.       TRAINING   AND   EXPERIMENTAL   DETAILS

                                                                   A.       Optimizer   and   VMC   Objective

   All   reported   NucleoNet   calculations   use   the   SPRING   optimizer   during   the   VMC   stage.     SPRING   [1]   is   an   eﬀi-
cient   stochastic-reconfiguration-based   optimizer   that   combines   ideas   from   minimum-step   stochastic   reconfiguration
(MinSR)  and  the  randomized  block  Kaczmarz  method.   At  each  iteration,  Monte  Carlo  samples  define  an  underdeter-
mined  subsampled  SR  problem  through  centered  log-wavefunction  derivatives  and  local-energy  fluctuations.   SPRING
updates   an   approximate   SR   direction   by   projecting   the   previous   direction   toward   the   solution   of   the   current   sub-
sampled  problem,  thereby  reusing  information  from  previous  minibatches.   Tikhonov  damping,  a  decay  factor  on  the
previous   update   direction,   and   a   Euclidean   norm   constraint   are   used   for   stability,   while   the   update   is   computed   in
sample  space  without  constructing  the  full  parameter-space  SR  matrix.
   For  a  wavefunction Ψθ ,  the  VMC  objective  is
                                                                             D       ˆHΨθE
                                                                               Ψθ
                                                                  Eθ =          ⟨Ψθ|Ψθ⟩     = Er∼|Ψθ|2 [EL(r)] ,                                                                                                              (17)


where  the  local  energy  is


                                                                                    EL(r) =  ˆHΨθ(r)Ψθ(r)  .                                                                                                                                             (18)


   The  corresponding  VMC  gradient  can  be  written  as

                                                          ∇θEθ = 2Er∼|Ψθ|2 [(EL(r) − Eθ) ∇θ log |Ψθ(r)|] .                                                                                               (19)

   At  each  iteration,  Monte  Carlo  samples  define  an  underdetermined  subsampled  SR  problem  through  centered  log-
wavefunction  derivatives  and  local-energy  fluctuations.

                                                                    C.       Hartree–Fock   Pretraining

   We  tested  Hartree–Fock  (HF)  pretraining  as  an  initialization  strategy  before  VMC  optimization.   During  pretraining,
the  neural  orbitals  are  fitted  to  Hartree–Fock  orbitals,  and  the  pretraining  parameters  are  updated  with  Adam.
   For  all  equilibrium  small-molecule  benchmarks,  HF  pretraining  was  used  before  VMC  optimization.   Although  the
final   optimized   energies   are   largely   determined   by   the   subsequent   VMC   stage,   HF   initialization   provides   a   stable
starting  point  and  follows  the  common  practice  in  neural  variational  Monte  Carlo  calculations.
   For  the  multi-geometry  benchmarks,  HF  pretraining  was  also  used  for  the  N2   dissociation  and  LiCN  displacement
experiments.    For   ethane   and   formamide,   HF   pretraining   resulted   in   slightly   higher   final   VMC   energies   than   direct
VMC  optimization  without  pretraining.   The  reported  calculations  therefore  omit  HF  pretraining  for  these  two  systems.

                                                                      VI.       ABLATION   DETAILS

   This   section   defines   the   ablation   variants   and   the   relative   metrics   used   in   the   main   text.      The   full   NucleoNet
architecture  consists  of  the  shared  electron–nucleus  backbone  together  with  an  electron–nucleus  envelope  conditioned
on   the   final   nuclear   representations.     We   consider   two   controlled   modifications   of   this   architecture.     NucleoNet-FE
removes   the   dependence   of   the   envelope   parameters   on   the   final   nuclear   representations   while   retaining   the   shared
electron–nucleus  backbone.   NucleoNet-FE-EB  is  constructed  from  NucleoNet-FE  by  further  restricting  the  backbone
to  the  electron  streams  while  retaining  static  nuclear  geometry  through  pooled  electron–nucleus  initialization  features
and  nuclear-centered  envelope  factors.   All  other  training,  sampling,  and  optimization  settings  are  kept  unchanged.

                                                                         A.       Relative   Metrics

   For   the   single-geometry   calculations,   the   energy   difference   of   an   ablation   model M   relative   to   the   full   NucleoNet
model  is  defined  as
                                                                      ∆EFull(M) = EM − EFull,                                                                                                                                 (24)
where  a  positive  value  indicates  a  higher  variational  energy  than  the  full  model.
   For  the  N2   sparse-geometry  ablation,  the  mean  absolute  error  of  model M   on  a  geometry  set S   is
                                                         MAES(M) =   1              X     |EM(Rs) − Eref(Rs)| .                                                                                                     (25)
                                                                               |S|  s∈S

For  N2, Eref   is  the  same  dissociation-limit-anchored  MLR4(6, 8)  reference  defined  in  Sec.                                        IV A.
   The  corresponding  change  relative  to  the  full  NucleoNet  model  is
                                                       ∆MAEFull,S(M) = MAES(M) − MAES(Full).                                                                                                 (26)
   We  report  this  quantity  separately  on  the  six  geometries  included  in  the  sparse-geometry  optimization,  denoted  by
Strain,  and  on  the  quantitative  evaluation  set,  denoted  by Seval.   The  metric  on Strain  measures  the  error  at  the  training
geometries,   whereas   the   metric   on Seval   measures   the   error   over   the   evaluation   set.    Positive   values   of ∆MAEFull,S
indicate  a  larger  MAE  than  the  full  NucleoNet  model.


                                                      B.       Fixed-envelope   ablation:    NucleoNet-FE

   In   the   full   NucleoNet   model,   the   envelope   amplitude   and   decay   parameters   are   generated   from   the   final   nuclear
representations  separately  for  each  determinant  and  spin  channel.   NucleoNet-FE  retains  the  same  isotropic  electron–
nucleus  envelope  form  and  the  same  determinant-  and  spin-resolved  parameterization,  but  removes  the  dependence  of
the  envelope  parameters  on  the  final  nuclear  representations.   The  envelope  parameters  are  instead  optimized  directly
as  geometry-independent  trainable  parameters,

                                     Ωkσ,FEia       = X     ¯πkσIa exp −¯λkσIa ∥ri − RI∥ ,       i ∈ Eσ,    a = 1, . . . , N,                                                               (27)
                                                     I∈N
where  ¯πkσIa     and  ¯λkσIa    are   geometry-independent   trainable   parameters   for   nucleus  I ,    orbital  a,    determinant  k,    and
spin   channel σ.     These   parameters   are   optimized   directly   and   do   not   depend   on   the   final   nuclear   representations.
The   nuclear   coordinates RI   remain   explicitly   present   through   the   electron–nucleus   distances,   so   the   fixed   envelope
retains  its  nuclear-centered  spatial  dependence.   All  other  components,  including  the  shared  electron–nucleus  backbone,
orbital  construction,  determinant  expansion,  and  spin-resolved  envelope  structure,  remain  unchanged.   The  comparison
between  NucleoNet  and  NucleoNet-FE  therefore  isolates  the  contribution  of  conditioning  the  envelope  parameters  on
the  learned  nuclear  representations.


                                            C.       Electron-only-backbone   ablation:    NucleoNet-FE-EB

   NucleoNet-FE-EB  is  constructed  from  NucleoNet-FE  by  restricting  the  backbone  to  the  electron  streams.   Accord-
