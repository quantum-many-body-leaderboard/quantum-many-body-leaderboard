In the supplementary materials, we provide more detailed results of the t-J model at ¯nh=1/8 doping.
A. Calculation details
We provide here more details of the calculations of the model via fPEPS method. 1 We start with the fPEPS wave
function composed of L1 ×L2 independent random tensors, to ensure that our calculations are unbiased. The fPEPS
wave functions are ﬁrst optimized via an imaginary time evolution with simple update (SU) scheme, 2 followed by
gradient optimization (GO) combined with Monte Carlo sampling techniques. 1,3 U(1) symmetry is enforced during
the calculations to conserve the number of electrons in the system.
In the GO, we start the optimization usingDc=2D. The GO step length is chosen to beτ=0.002. For each GO step,
we use 20000 sweeps of MC samplings, where a sweep is deﬁned as attempts to update the physical conﬁgurations of
all sites sequentially. We gradually decrease GO step length τ to 0.001, and at the same time increase Dc to Dc=56
(∼4.7D), and MC sampling number to 200000 sweeps per GO step. We optimize the energies until they are stable.
Finally, we calculate the energies and correlation functions using 400000 MC sweeps. In the calculations, the errors
from Dc are less than 10−5 (more details of the convergence on Dc can be found on the Ref.1) and the errors from
MC sampling are about 10−5.
B. Compare with the exact results on the 4 ×4 lattice
We compare the hole density and local magnetization of the 4 ×4 lattice obtained by the fPEPS and exact
diagonalization calculations in Fig. S1. The local magnetization is less than 10 −4 from fPEPS simulations compared
with the exact result of 0. The ground state energy obtained by fPEPS is EfPEPS=-0.56428, compared with the
exact energy of EED=-0.56436. They are in remarkable good agreement both for the hole/spin distributions and the
energies.

C. The ground state energies and hole-spin distributions on lattices of diﬀerent sizes
We have performed the fPEPS simulations on a various of lattices sites ranging from the smallest one of 4 ×4 to
the maximum size of 10×16 and the ground state conﬁgurations are summarized in Table S1. A unform phase is seen
in the 4 ×4 lattice. However, for a 4 ×8 lattice, the ground state become non-uniform. Further increases the lattices
size, we see the ground states become diagonal stripes, with width of 4 unit cells, except for the extreme case of 3 ×32
lattice where vertical stripes are seen. The hole and spin density distributions are shown for the 4×8, 8×10 and 10×16
lattices in Fig. S2. These results suggest that the diagonal stripes are quite robust for diﬀerent sizes of the systems.
The ground state energies for selected systems in the diagonal stripe phase are given in Table S2. The standard
deviation of the energies are around 10 −5. The ground state energy in the thermodynamic limit is obtained by a
second-order polynomial extrapolation.

D. The ground state hole distributions of with anisotropic interactions
We also simulate the t-J models with anisotropic interaction following the Ref.4 on diﬀerent lattices sizes. We use
the parameters Jx = 0.4tx, ty = 0.85tx and Jy = 0.289tx and the results are shown in Fig. S3 for a 8 ×8 and a 8 ×12
lattice. We ﬁnd that the ground states have vertical stripes along the direction with stronger interactions (i.e., x
direction), which are in agreement with the results of Ref.4.
