                 The spin-1/2 kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet is a central model in the study of quantum spin liquids, yet
                 its ground-state  properties remain unresolved.   We perform large-scale  variational Monte-Carlo calculations
                 using neural-network quantum states (NQSs) with kagome-geometry-aware message-passing convolution on
                 two-dimensional tori containing up to 300 spins,  and achieve exceptionally low variational energies for the
                 kagome model.   The spin and dimer structure factors show no evidence of magnetic or valence-bond long-
                 range order. Moreover, the dominant symmetry-resolved spin and dimer correlations exhibit a common scaling
                 behavior consistent with the emergentSO(6)monopole multiplet of the Dirac spin liquid. Finally, the fidelity
                 of the optimized NQSs with Gutzwiller-projected mean-field states increasingly favors the Dirac state over the
                 competingZ                  2 candidates as the system size grows. Together, these complementary results provide evidence for
                 aU(1)Dirac spin-liquid ground state in the kagome Heisenberg antiferromagnet.

                                                      D.    Connection to the QED3 monopole multiplet

   Next, we discuss why the two symmetry channels singled out above are particularly relevant for the Dirac-spin-liquid inter-
pretation.  The low-energy theory of the kagomeU(1)DSL isN                         f =4QED     3.  The six lowest charge-one monopole operators
transform in the antisymmetric representation6of SU(4), or equivalently as a vector of the emergent SO(6) symmetry.  They
decompose into three spin-singlet and three spin-triplet components. Their lattice quantum numbers on the kagome lattice were
clarified in Ref. [3].  In particular, the spin-triplet monopoles have zero crystal momentum and angular momentuml=2under
C6, identifying them with theΓ-E          2 spin sector considered above.
   The spin-singlet monopoles instead carry the translation quantum numbers of the threeMpoints and correspond to VBS fluc-
tuations. Matching the translation and little-group transformation laws in the Table 3 of Ref. [3] to the bond-space representation
constructed above identifies the mirror-odd component with theM-F           2 sector. This observation is useful because the symmetry
resolution separates the physical dimer response into operators with different continuum quantum numbers, which are mixed
together in the scalar dimer structure factorS         Df (q).

same scaling dimension∆                  Φ. In the continuum theory their leading two-point function takes the form
